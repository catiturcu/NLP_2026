task2 — Generalized Romanian N-gram Language Model
Preprocessing: The text is converted to lowercase, split into sentences using ., ! and ?, and the remaining punctuation is replaced with spaces. Tokens are extracted using split(). Sentence boundaries are marked with <s> and </s>.
The program allows the user to choose N and calculates N-gram counts and conditional probabilities using Laplace smoothing.
Corpus statistics:
- Number of tokens: 14,333 (including sentence-boundary markers)
- Vocabulary size: 3,180 (including sentence-boundary markers)
- Selected N: 3
- Number of distinct N-grams: 11,993
Interpretation: The N-gram model estimates word-sequence probabilities from the Romanian corpus. Laplace smoothing assigns non-zero probabilities to unseen N-grams.
task3— Probability of a New Romanian Sentence
Input sentence: ana are mere
N = 3
Conditional probabilities:
('<s>', 'ana', 'are') -> 0.0003144654
('ana', 'are', 'mere') -> 0.0003144654
('are', 'mere', '</s>') -> 0.0003144654
Sentence probability: 3.109701048267784e-11
Log-probability: -24.193909427322666
Interpretation: The sentence probability is calculated by multiplying the conditional probabilities of its trigrams. The log-probability is the sum of their logarithms and is useful for avoiding numerical underflow when processing longer sentences.

Comparison with the N-gram Model
DistilGPT2 predicts `to better understand` based on patterns learned during pretraining. In contrast, the N-gram model from Task 2 predicts the next word based on conditional probabilities calculated from the Romanian training corpus.
The neural model uses a pretrained model learned from a much larger text collection, while the N-gram model relies on word-sequence frequencies in the collected Romanian corpus. Therefore, their predictions may differ. Since the example is in English and the N-gram corpus is Romanian, this comparison is illustrative rather than a direct experimental comparison.
